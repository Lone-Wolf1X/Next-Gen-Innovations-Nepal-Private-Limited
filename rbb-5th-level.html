Examination Scheme

The examination is divided into two phases:

  • First Phase: Written Examination (200 Marks)
  • Second Phase: Interview (30 Marks)

Paper I: Economics, Banking and General Knowledge

Full Marks: 100 | Pass Marks: 40

Section A (50 Marks): Objective Multiple Choice Questions (50 Questions x 1 Mark) - Time: 45 Minutes

Section B (50 Marks): Subjective Short Answers (10 Questions x 5 Marks) - Time: 1 Hour 15 Minutes

Syllabus Breakdown (Section A):

  • 1. Basic Economics: Scarcity of Resources, Demand/Supply, Cost/Benefit, Market Structure, Nepalese Economy, Monetary/Fiscal Policy.
  • 2. Financial Market: Capital Market, Money Market, Repo Rate, Open Market Operations, SLR, CRR, OTC.
  • 3. Financial Institutions: Commercial/Dev Banks, Finance/Microfinance, Insurance, EPF, CIT, SSF.

Syllabus Breakdown (Section B):

  • 4. Digital/Electronic Payment: IPS, ConnectIPS, RTGS, SWIFT, Internet/Mobile Banking, QR, NFC, CBDC.
  • 5. Banking Terminology: Capital Fund, LLP, CD Ratio, Trade Finance (LC/BG), Base Rate, KYC, Audit.
  • 6. General Knowledge: Countries/Currencies, Top Economies, Trade Routes, Nepal's History/Geography/Agriculture/Tourism.

Paper II: Governance, Cash and Vault Management

Full Marks: 100 | Pass Marks: 40

Format: Subjective (Short Answers: 12x5 marks, Long Answers: 4x10 marks) - Time: 2 Hours 30 Minutes

Syllabus Breakdown (Section A - 50 Marks):

  • 1. Daily Cash Handling: Security Measures, Cash Counting, Fund Transfer, Clean Note Policy.
  • 2. Customer Service: Customer Satisfaction, Signature Verification, Front Desk, Grievance Handling.
  • 3. Banking Laws: NRB Act 2058, BAFIA 2073, Payment/Settlement Act, Money Laundering Prevention, Banking Offence.

Syllabus Breakdown (Section B - 50 Marks):

  • 4. Gold/Silver Loan Transactions: Purity Test, Spot Price, LTV Ratio, Margin Call.
  • 5. Accounting and Vault Management: Vault Key Handling, CCTV, Day-end Recording.
  • 6. Other Laws/Policies: Companies Act, Labour Act, Public Procurement, Right to Information, RBB Employee Service Rules.

Theoretical Notes & History

1. Nepalese Economy: Status and Prospects

The Nepalese economy is largely dependent on agriculture and remittances. Remittances contribute to over a quarter of the GDP, making it a crucial component of macroeconomic stability. However, this reliance also makes the economy vulnerable to external shocks.

Challenges: Trade deficit, lack of industrialization, infrastructure bottlenecks, and political instability impacting long-term economic policies.

2. Banking History of Nepal

The formal banking system in Nepal started with the establishment of Nepal Bank Limited in 1937 AD (1994 BS). Later, Nepal Rastra Bank was established in 1956 AD (2013 BS) as the central bank under the NRB Act 1955. Rastriya Banijya Bank was established in 1966 AD (2022 BS).

3. Key Concepts

  • Repo Rate: The rate at which the central bank lends money to commercial banks in the event of any shortfall of funds.
  • KYC (Know Your Customer): The process of a business verifying the identity of its clients and assessing their suitability, along with the potential risks of illegal intentions towards the business relationship.
